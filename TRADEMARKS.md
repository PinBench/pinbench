# Trademark policy

The **Apache-2.0 licence covers the code, not the name.** Section 6 of
Apache-2.0 explicitly grants no trademark rights. This document states what
you may and may not do with the project's name and marks.

## Marks covered

- The name **"PinBench"**
- The project logo and app icon
- Any confusingly similar name or mark

These are trademarks of Burhan Khanzada, whether or not registered.

## What you may do without asking

- Say that your software **works with**, **is built on**, **integrates
  with**, or **is a fork of** PinBench.
- Use the name in blog posts, tutorials, videos, courses, talks, and
  academic papers, including commercially.
- Use the name in a repository description, README, or package description
  that accurately describes the relationship.
- Redistribute **unmodified** builds under the project name.
- Use the logo to link to the official project.

## What requires permission

- Naming your **fork or derivative product** "PinBench"
  or anything confusingly similar (e.g. "PinBench Pro", "PinBench Plus",
  "PinBnch"). Name your fork something distinctly your own.
- Distributing **modified** builds under the project name or logo. If you
  change the code, change the name — users must be able to tell whose
  software they are running.
- Using the marks in a way that implies official endorsement, affiliation,
  or that yours is the official distribution.
- Using the marks in a domain name, app-store listing, social media
  handle, or company name.
- Selling merchandise bearing the marks.

## Why this exists

Open source without a trademark policy has a specific failure mode: someone
takes the code, adds spyware or a scam payment flow, ships it under the same
name in an app store, and every support request and every bad review lands
on the original maintainer. The trademark is the one tool that stops that,
and it is fully compatible with the code being free. Forking is welcome and
always will be — just do it under your own name.
